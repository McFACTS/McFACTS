# Summary

Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) are gas-rich and dynamically complex structures at
the center of some galaxies. At the core of an AGN exists a supermassive black
hole (SMBH), creating an immense gravitational well hosting a diverse population
of black holes and stars that collectively form a nuclear star cluster (NSC). A 
portion of the gravitational wave (GW) events detected by the 
LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) detector network are expected to originate from the 
merging of binary black holes (BBHs) that formed while embedded in the 
accretion disk of AGN. Some of these BBHs are expected to be detectable by the 
Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) GW observatory launching in the 
2030s. The detectable population of these objects form the "AGN Channel" and 
strongly depend on the properties of the accretion disk, NSC, and other 
parameters of the host galaxy. Some of these properties are difficult to measure 
directly, but can be indirectly constrained by comparing real populations to 
synthetic catalogs generated by simulations dependent on our parameterized 
understanding of the universe.

`McFACTS` (Monte carlo For AGN Channel Testing and Simulation) is the leading 
open-source population synthesis code modeling parts of the AGN channel 
[@mckernan:2025]. Given an AGN disk model and several properties defining the
SMBH and NSC, `McFACTS` seeds a population of single black holes and stars. 
This initial population is evolved through a variety of physical effects, 
resulting in some of the objects forming binaries. The gas in the disk 
drives accretion effects, such as migration and eccentricity dampening for
single objects and binaries. Objects in the disk and NSC undergo dynamical 
encounters and exchange energy. The exchanged energy modifies the orbital 
parameters of single objects around the SMBH and of binary objects with respect
to their center of mass. Binaries significantly softened by these 
interactions are ionized. Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals (EMRIs), single 
objects orbiting the SMBH with a mass ratio 
$q$ of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-7}$, BBH, and stellar binaries all evolve through 
GW decay. `McFACTS` makes use of several packages commonly used in the 
scientific python ecosystem, such as `Astropy` [@astropy:2022], 
`NumPy` [@harris:2020], and `SciPy` [@scipy:2020]. The AGN disk structure 
and properties are obtained through the `pAGN` [@gangardt:2024] package, 
supporting both the Sirko and Goodman [@sg:2003] and Thompson, Quataert and 
Murry [@tqm:2005] disk models.

In relation to the version of `McFACTS` published in @mckernan:2025, this paper
primarily documents the substantial restructuring and addition of new 
features to the code since version `0.1.0`. The restructure is a complete 
refactor of the simulation framework to follow a modular design, allowing for 
the intuitive and dynamic composition of simulation timelines. Several key 
methods used throughout the code have been vectorized and computationally
demanding algorithms have been migrated to a companion library written in rust 
called `McFAST`. 

Several new astrophysical features are expanded on in works by members of the 
`McFACTS` collaboration and aren't fully discussed in this paper: a. Models 
and methods for evaluating the electromagnetic counterparts of mergers 
[@mcpike:2026; @mcpike:2026b, in prep]. b. Determination of merger remnant 
properties via a numerical relativity surrogate model [@ray:2026]. c. 
Application of semi-analytical gas evolution models for binary objects 
[@postiglione:2026, in prep]. 


# Statement of need

Of the proposed BBH formation channels, AGN remain poorly constrained. 
A population synthesis code for the AGN channel must therefore make it possible 
to easily vary physical assumptions and prescriptions, while remaining fast and 
performant. The original release of `McFACTS` demonstrated these 
capabilities and established the scientific value of an open-source AGN channel 
code where one did not previously exist. This inaugural version concentrated 
the simulation logic and general bookkeeping into one main script, making new
contributions difficult to add and different combinations of physical processes
hard to test. With the continued growth of the `McFACTS` collaboration, the 
limited flexibility of the main script became a significant obstacle in 
maintaining and upgrading the code.

The restructured version of `McFACTS`, documented in this paper, address these
needs directly. Utilities and code for handling physical processes now 
exist as independent and reusable modular units. New classes representing 
the astrophysical objects within a simulation are stored and managed in a 
central location with automatic duplicate and consistency checks against the 
entire datastore. The input/output is centralized and upgraded to use 
exchangeable handlers, each supporting different file types and improving 
interoperability with other codes and data processing pipelines. Several 
compute-heavy functions run as external methods implemented in rust, with 
verified data parity. The result of all of these changes is a version of 
`McFACTS` that is fast to run, simple to support, and more adoptable by 
collaborators looking to extend the functionality of `McFACTS` or integrate 
it into their work.


# State of the field            

Several population synthesis codes targeting BBH formation and
evolution currently exist, include `COSMIC` [@breivik:2020], 
`COMPAS` [@riley:2022], `CMC` [@rodriguez:2022], and `cogsworth` [@wagg:2024].
These codes focus on formation channels for BBHs in gas-poor environments (e.
g. isolated binaries, dense stellar clusters, etc.), but do not model the 
critical gas-rich dynamics of an AGN disk. Codes that focused on the AGN 
channel have historically been geared towards specific features, such as 
focusing on the mergers found at migration traps, or are not open source 
and have not been released publicly. `McFACTS` distinguishes itself from 
its predecessors by being public, open-source, and tooled to simulate the 
AGN channel for BBH mergers across a broad range of the AGN parameter space.

# Software design

The restructure of `McFACTS` separates the framework from any particular
simulation or model assembled out of its building blocks. This new framework
is best understood through a top-down lens, where each level orchestrates the
level beneath it. A `Galaxy` holds its populations in a `FilingCabinet` of 
`AGNObjectArray`s. The `AGNOBjectArray`s are evolved according to a
`SimulationTimeline`, and timeline is composed of individual `TimelineActor`s.
The population is seeded with `Populator` objects.

![Diagram showing a high level overview of the relationship between different object types used by the restructured version of `McFACTS`.](restructure-diag.png){#diag height="750px"}

## Galaxy

The `Galaxy` object sits at the core of the simulation framework. A single
`Galaxy` object represents one synthetic galaxy, with its own population,
deterministic random state using a Philox pseudo-random number generator, and the history of simulation
timelines that have been run (graphically represented in \autoref{diag}). A 
`Galaxy` is first populated using `Populator`objects. Once the `Galaxy` is 
seeded, a `SimulationTimeline` holding several `TimelineActor` objects is run.

## Filing Cabinet and AGN Object Array

A `FilingCabinet` is a flexible typed-container that stores the population for 
a single `Galaxy` object or an entire universe of galaxies. The cabinet stores a
dictionary of `AGNObjectArray`s referenced by a string key. The `FilingCabinet`
also provides a storage interface for variables that need to be accessed by
multiple different modules without introducing circular imports or issues that 
arise from module interdependency. The `FilingCabinet` and `AGNObjectArray` 
objects feature toggleable consistency checks to ensure an object with the same 
`UUID` does not appear in two categories at once. The `AGNObjectArray` object
allows for the association of attributes representing physical properties of 
objects, while maintaining the pythonic convention of storing object 
properties (orbital parameters, mass, spin and spin angle, etc.) in parallel 
`NumPy` arrays. Subclasses for different population categories, such as single
black holes, BBHs, merged black holes, and so forth, allow for quick 
object-type validation when needed, and the inheritance of shared attributes.


## Simulation Timeline, Timeline Actors, and Populators

Evolution of the population in a `Galaxy` is scheduled through a 
`SimulationTimeline` object. The timeline holds an ordered list of different 
operations that are applied to the population over some fixed number of 
timesteps. A single `Galaxy` can run several different timelines 
sequentially, processing the population and other parameters in distinct 
stages. The `TimelineActor` object encapsulates the implementation of 
physical processes through a common `perform` interface, with each actor 
generally representing a single physical process (migration, accretion, 
eccentricity damping, binary formation, merger processing, etc.). Each 
actor is passed a reference to the `FilingCabinet` of that galaxy, and can
read and written directly to it. A `Populator` is structured similarly to a
`TimelineActor`, but distinctly plays the role of seeding the initial 
populations, constructing the starting `AGNObjectArray` objects passed to the
filling cabinet.


## Crosscutting Utilities

Several utilities are used across the entire hierarchy of the simulation 
framework. The `SettingsManager` acts as a central repo for built-in defaults
and user overridable settings. Internal constants are protected from
modification by the user, and custom settings can be passed for use with
third-party modules. The `SnapshotHandler` provides an interface for the 
simulation framework to load and save `SettingsManager` and `FilingCabinet`
objects. By default, we support both `.txt` and `.ini` for the loading and
saving of the `SettingsManger` and support `.txt` files for the `FilingCabinet`. 
Members of the `McFACTS` collaboration have developed tooling to support `.hdf5`
files and the HDF5 format.
There are also plans to supportwith `.parquet` files using the Apache Parquet 
format and  `.db, .sqlite` files through SQLite. 
This flexibility in IO formating allows for a highly customizable
experience, encouraging the implementation of `McFACTS` in to third-party data 
processing pipelines and software stacks.


## Extension: McFAST

`mcfast` is a custom-built extension to the `McFACTS` codebase written in Rust,
with Python bindings produced by `pyo3`. It provides optimized variants of
several `McFACTS` functions, including vectorized, single-pass variants of
computationally heavy functions (e.g. `shock_luminosity`,
`analytical_kick_velocity`) and tailored variants of unit conversion 
functions (e.g. `si_from_r_g`, `r_g_from_units`) which bypass AstroPy's unit 
conversion operations.


### Motivation

As of the version v0.3.0 of `McFACTS`, the most time-intensive operations fell
into three categories:
 1. Long chains of `numpy` array operations on several arrays of equal length.
 2. Inefficient general purpose code in Python (eigenvalue rootfinding vs 
    Cardano, `np.where` vs `np.searchsort()`, etc.).
 3. Small, but frequently-called, helpers reliant on AstroPy's unit 
    conversion operations.

`mcfast` aims to mitigate slowdowns as a result of problems 1 and 3, both of
which would benefit from a purpose-built, compiled extensions.


### Design

A primary goal in the development of the `mcfast` extensions is to minimize 
data transfer across the Python-Rust Foreign Function Interface (FFI). The 
input-output of candidate functions in `McFACTS` are numpy arrays, so 
`mcfast` makes heavy use of zero-copy reads using the `numpy` crate API
(similar to Cython's typed array views). Output `numpy` arrays are allocated
from inside the Rust extension, passing only pointers across the FFI boundary
without copying data.

In addition to converting array-heavy workloads into pre-compiled, single-pass
operations, some variant functions also make use of iterator-level parallelism
using the `rayon` crate. This functionality is under review and may be removed
in the future in order to not interfere with per-galaxy threading in the
restructure.

With the `mcfast` helper variants, individual functions have gained speedups in
the 10x to 200x range on local runs, contributing heavily to the overall ~6x
improvement in total simulation runtime achieved since the release of v0.3.0.


### Language Choice

Rust was selected as the compiled language for the `mcfast` extension due to
several benefits over languages like C or C++. Rust provides strong safeguards
against memory unsafe operations, with built-in scoping capabilities to handle 
memory ownership and lifetime. Rust compiles directly to static binaries, which 
can easily be wrapped through Python wheels using `pyo3` bindings and 
the `maturin` build system. Rust also provides a strong, static, and expressive
type system, allowing for the extension to ensure the proper typing of incoming
and outgoing values.


### Python Function Parity

`mcfast` variant functions are tested against the original python functions in
`McFACTS`, ensuring that both functions produce the same result. Where an exact
match can't be guaranteed between the results, a tolerance of at least 1e-6 is
enforced for floating point operations.


# Research impact statement

Since its initial release, `McFACTS` has been used as the primary research 
tool in several publications aimed at studying the AGN channel. Collectively,
these publications are cited over 100 times, and provided a wealth of insight 
into how the AGN channel contributes to the population of detectible sources
for LIGO-Virgo-KAGARA and LISA. Papers published by the `McFACTS` 
collaboration study the effects of AGN on the q vs. chi-effective 
distribution of LVK detectible BBH mergers [@cook:2025], show the effects 
of NSC properties, galaxy mass, and redshift across an entire population of 
galaxies [@delfavero:2025], demonstrate the effects of the AGN channel on 
electromagnetic signatures and remnant kicks of embedded BBH mergers 
[@mcpike:2026], and explore the AGN channel's effects on the resulting 
properties of BBH merger remnants [@ray:2026] through numerical relativity 
surrogate models.
